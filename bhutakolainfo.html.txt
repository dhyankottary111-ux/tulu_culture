<section id="origin" class="section">
  <h2>Origin of <span class="blue">Bhuta Kola</span></h2>
  <p>
    Bhuta Kola is an ancient spirit worship ritual practiced in Tulunadu,
    where divine spirits known as <strong>Daivas</strong> are invoked to
    protect villages, families, and land.
  </p>
  <p>
    Unlike temple-centric traditions, Bhuta Kola reflects a living belief
    system where the divine directly communicates through the performer.
  </p>
</section>

<section id="ritual" class="section soft">
  <h2>The <span class="blue">Ritual Process</span></h2>
  <p>
    The ritual is performed at night in sacred spaces called
    <strong>Daivasthana</strong>. A trained performer adorns elaborate
    costumes, facial paint, and ornaments.
  </p>
  <p>
    Through music, chants, and dance, the performer enters a trance
    believed to be possession by the Daiva.
  </p>
</section>

<section id="belief" class="section">
  <h2>Spiritual <span class="blue">Beliefs</span></h2>
  <p>
    The Daiva is believed to act as a guardian spirit, delivering justice,
    resolving disputes, and guiding the community.
  </p>
  <p>
    Devotees seek blessings for protection, prosperity, and harmony.
  </p>
</section>

<section id="significance" class="section soft">
  <h2>Cultural <span class="blue">Significance</span></h2>
  <p>
    Bhuta Kola represents a unique fusion of spirituality, social order,
    and environmental respect. It reinforces moral values and community unity.
  </p>
  <p>
    Even today, Bhuta Kola remains a powerful symbol of Tulunadu’s
    living heritage.
  </p>
</section>
